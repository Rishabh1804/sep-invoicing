### Three views over one store

**Day** is the entry surface: present / half / absent per worker, the area they worked, and
their own OT hours. **Week** is a Mon–Sat grid whose cells cycle on tap — for fixing what the
day view got wrong — with the `15/20` headcount row the daily relay already speaks in.
**Roster** is the master: comp class, rates, home area, on-floor flag.

### The roster ships empty

Names and wages are payroll data and this repo is public. Structure — the eight areas, the two
comp classes, the wage arithmetic — is code. The people are entered once on the device. That is
a deliberate departure from `SEED_CLIENTS`, which does carry real names; a client name is on
every invoice that leaves the building, a worker's day rate is not.

Consequence worth knowing: **GitHub sync and the JSON export now carry payroll.** Both go to
the owner's own repo and the owner's own file, the same as every invoice already does, and
credentials still live outside `S`. But the backup is a different kind of document than it was
yesterday.

## What "the extra" is

This was the open question, and the daily sheet answers it.

Shyam's relay books hours two ways. Named men carry their own out-time — that is overtime, per
worker, at their hour rate. But every day also carries lines like `EXTRA 16 HOURS` written
against an **area block** — the barrel line, the 6 AM VAT slot — with nobody attached. They are
real paid hours at the contract tier and the payout sheet settles them.

So the app records them as exactly that: **hours booked to an area, unattributed.** They are
counted in the bill, reported on their own row, and the card names their share of the total.

They are **never spread across the men present.** Spreading would produce a per-worker cost
that reads precise and is invented — and worse, it would answer the fixed-versus-variable
question by accident, in whichever direction the blend happened to fall. That question is the
whole reason this tab exists.

## Fixed against variable, and why it is two numbers

**Fixed** is the permanent payroll. It accrues by calendar day whether or not anybody typed the
day. **Variable** is contract days, rest credit, named OT, and the extra.

The SSS Mehta decision turns on precisely this split: at fixed labour that account still
contributes ₹0.53/kg; at volume-scaling labour it loses ₹1.64/kg. A single blended labour
number picks a side silently. Two numbers make the reader pick, which is correct — the app
cannot know how the contract labour bill behaves at lower tonnage, and pretending otherwise
would be the most expensive rounding error in this codebase.

The arithmetic is the ratified rule: `(days worked + rest credit) × ₹/day + OT × 1.1`, rest
credit gated on a full week, half a day counting half. Every constant sits in Settings.

## Where the hours went

The other half of the open question was **allocation**. The card ranks variable labour by the
area each day and hour was worked in — barrel, pickling, the two VAT lines, colour, and the
off-floor pair — by cost rather than by headcount, because an area that pulls the overtime is
more expensive than one that merely has bodies standing in it. Off-floor areas are shaded apart
so a reader scanning for where the plant's money goes does not have to remember which is which.

Permanent payroll is **not** in that breakdown, and the caption says so. A monthly salary is not
attributable to a day, let alone to the area that day was worked in; splitting it by home area
would print an allocation nobody measured. Drawn with the existing `chartRankedBars` rather than
a new bar shape — Top Items is the view a reader arrives from.

## What the card refuses to say

Four states, not three: **unmarked is not absent.** A row nobody has reached costs nothing; an
absence costs a day's wage and has to be said. One level up, a day with no attendance key is a
day nobody typed, which is not a day nobody worked.

That distinction is what the coverage line is for, and it is not neutral in either direction:

- Permanent salary accrues across days nobody typed; contract wages, OT and extra do not. So
  **an incomplete range reads low, never neutral** — and the card says so in place, every time,
  complete or not. A card that only mentions its gaps when it has them teaches the reader to
  stop looking for the line.
- ₹/kg is **withheld below 90% of working days** — the same bar the client realisation table
  uses, for the same reason.
- ₹/kg is also withheld under a fortnight, but for a different reason: not enough of either
  side to divide. The lag between plating and billing cannot be gated away at *any* length —
  tonnage here is billed tonnage — so a range under two months carries that caveat beside the
  figure rather than pretending the threshold fixed it.
- A ₹/kg drawn over partial tonnage coverage reads **high**, the opposite direction from
  realisation, because tonnage is the denominator here and the numerator there. The card states
  the direction rather than leaving the reader to work it out.

Deletion of a worker is refused while attendance names them. Removing the row would not remove
the marks, it would orphan them — every past week's labour would quietly drop that wage and no
figure would say why. Clearing Active keeps the history and takes them out of today's
denominator, which is what "left" actually means.

## Tests

15 new specs (`p24-attendance-labour.spec.ts`), 201 in the suite. They pin the arithmetic —
a half day is half a day, 4 h of OT is ₹209 and not ₹190, 10 extra hours are ₹475 — and they
pin the refusals, which is the part that would rot quietly: the withheld ₹/kg asserts on
`toBeVisible()`, not on `toContainText` alone, because a reporting path that only proves its
message reached the DOM is the trap the certificate run's toast fell into.

`workingDaysBack()` joins `todayIso()` / `recentTs()` in the fixtures. Coverage is measured as
recorded working days over working days in range, so a fixture that seeds a Sunday or skips a
Tuesday does not read as fully covered — and the ₹/kg under test is then withheld for a reason
the spec never asked about. Same class of trap as a hardcoded date.
