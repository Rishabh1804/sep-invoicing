## Why this exists

PR #28 shipped the Staff tab with two comp classes, `permanent` and `contract`, built from the
ratified rule quoted in the codex: `(days worked + rest credit) × ₹/day + OT × 1.1`.

That rule is real. It governs **one** of the shop's three pay mechanics. Reading the payout
slips rather than the summary of them shows the other two:

- The salaried tier is **not on a flat monthly**. `2026-04-05-backlog-new-rates.md` is a rate
  card in **₹/day** — Shyam 576, Sarat 500, Rupa 500, Sunil 470, Suklal 440, Lakhi 420, Bhanu
  410, Lal 360, Uday 300 — and the monthly figure is derived from it, not the other way round.
  A flat salary divided by calendar days matches no slip and, worse, does not move when
  somebody is absent, which is exactly what the attendance gate exists to make it do.
- The weekly pool has **no day rate at all**. W32 and W33 pay every hour at a flat ₹47.50:
  Sambhu 63 h → ₹2,992, Rakesh 80 h → ₹3,800, Sripati 16 h → ₹760. Charging a day rate and
  paying the rest as overtime at ×1.1 invents a boundary the slip does not have and overstates
  the overtime by a tenth.

Seeding real workers into the model as it stood would have produced confidently wrong figures
in both tiers — the one failure this feature exists to prevent.

## What shipped

**Three comp classes**, each matching a mechanic somebody is actually paid under.

| | Paid | Rest days | Overtime |
|---|---|---|---|
| `monthly` | ₹/day × days worked | range's rest days × the attendance gate | day rate ÷ 8 × 1.1 |
| `hourly` | every hour at one flat rate | — | none |
| `daily` | ₹/day × days worked | one day per full week | hour rate × the multiplier |

`daily` is the retired `contract` class under a name that no longer claims to describe this
shop's pool. No SEP tier is on it; it is kept because it is the shape most job-work contracts
take.

**The three-layer rest gate.** Rest days are paid, scaled by the worker's own attendance: at or
above 90% all of them, above 80% half, below that none. Both thresholds are in Settings. It is
exact over a calendar month — the period it was written for — and over a shorter range it judges
each rest day on that range's attendance alone, which the card states rather than hides.

**Hours are captured for the hourly pool.** Their row takes hours, not overtime, and offers
Present and Absent only: a half day is not a state an hourly worker can be in, because the hours
already carry that granularity. The week grid's corner figure follows the same rule — the whole
day for the pool, the overtime on top for everyone else.

**Overtime derives from the day rate.** `₹/day ÷ 8`, then the multiplier. That is how the rate
card's own OT column is built, and deriving it means the two numbers cannot drift apart. An
explicit hour rate still overrides.

**A roster-scoped import.** Settings → Import replaces the whole state, so a roster file through
that door would take every invoice with it. `Staff → Roster → Import` merges by **name** and
touches nothing else on `S`. The name is the merge key rather than the id precisely so an
existing worker is updated in place and every attendance mark already hanging off their id
survives — two devices that typed the same person gave them different ids.

**A migration** carries `permanent` → `monthly` (dividing any flat salary by 30 to recover the
day rate the slips are written in) and `contract` → `daily`. Idempotent, and a no-op on the
overwhelmingly likely case, since the roster ships empty.

## The model reproduces a slip

The strongest thing in this diff is one spec. A W32 week's hourly pool foots to **₹27,549**
across ten hands and 580 hours; feeding those same hours through this model lands on
**₹27,550.00**, the rupee being the slip's own three half-rupee roundings taken down.

The fixture's names are generic and its hours are not. An hour count is not payroll, and the
point is that the *arithmetic* ties to a document somebody was paid against. A model that cannot
reproduce such a document is not one to price a decision with — and the previous model could
not have come close.

## Addendum — Staff → Areas (same day)

Asked for after the merge: a view of the floor by work area, to see over- and understaffing and
to test whether the extra hours correlate with what the owner observes on the floor.

**Placement.** Built as a fourth view inside Staff (Day / Week / **Areas** / Roster) rather than
a ninth bottom-nav tab. It reads the same attendance store — it is the people data seen by place
— and a ninth tab would put each target at ~43px on a 393px phone, under the 44px minimum this
app enforces everywhere else. Promoting it later is a one-line change in `body.html`, `init.js`
and `swipe.js`.

**Staffing.** Average heads per area over the recorded days, against a **complement the owner
sets**, editable in place, with the area's own median beside it so a target that was never true
shows as such. An area with no complement says *no complement* rather than reading overstaffed
against an implied zero. Heads come from the day's marks, so a worker lent elsewhere counts where
they actually stood; `flex` marks are reported as a named shortfall rather than distributed.

**The extra, checked.** Two instruments, and the honesty is the design:

- **Booked where nobody was marked** — extra hours against an area that had no one in it that
  day. The card names the area and the date. It is a flag on the *paperwork*: hours booked to the
  wrong area, an assignment nobody typed, and hours never worked are indistinguishable from here,
  and the card says so in place. When every booking is manned it says the check **passed** —
  a test that only speaks up on failure teaches the reader to distrust its silence.
- **Extra hours per head-day** — the area's extra over its worker-days, read against the hours
  those same people already logged. Labelled a **plausibility test, not an allocation**, because
  the wage arithmetic deliberately never spreads the extra and a diagnostic that looks like an
  allocation would quietly become one.

**What the existing slips already say.** On the two most recent weekly payouts the extra is
**28.5% of paid hours in W32** (231 h of 811, ₹10,972.50) and **27.0% in W33** (156 h of 578,
₹7,410) — ₹18,382.50 across a fortnight with no name against it. That is not evidence the hours
are wrong; it is evidence that a quarter of the contract wage bill rests on a line nothing
corroborates, which is what makes the cross-check worth having. T-CY in the codex is the same
question from the other end.

## Addendum 2 — the extra reconciled against the staffing norms

The Areas view shipped with the extra treated as **unattributable on principle**, and that
principle was wrong on the facts. `T-CG`, resolved on the Architect's own ruling of 11 Jun 2026,
defines it precisely:

> staffing norms **VAT A1 = 4 · VAT A2 = 4 · Barrel = 3 · Barrel pickling = 2 · VA1+A2 pickling
> = 3** … coverage is absorbed **collectively by the short sub-area's present crew, pro-rata** …
> fully person-attributable from the existing relay format. Payment mechanics: **pooled as a
> single record line; Shyam pays it pro-rata on the floor.**

The payout slips and the roster were searched; the attendance files, where the decode lives, were
not. Same failure as the comp model one addendum earlier — the summary read, the instrument not.

**What survives:** the ruling separates *pooled for payment* from *attributable for measurement*.
"Don't spread it in the wage arithmetic" stays right. "It can't be attributed at all" was wrong,
and the attribution the module refused to compute is one the codex explicitly asks for.

### What changed

**Areas realigned to the norm units.** Pickling split into `pickling-barrel` (2) and
`pickling-vat` (3); `colour` retired into VAT A1, being the passivation step rather than a place
with a crew. A migration re-points marks, home areas, extra bookings and complements, and logs
that any mark meaning the barrel side needs moving by hand — it lands on the VAT side and nothing
in the record can tell them apart after the fact.

**Expected vs booked.** `Σ max(0, norm − heads) × 8` per sub-area per day, against what was
tagged. Three disagreements reported apart, each with area and date: booked at or above
complement (the case the rule forbids), booked where nobody was marked, and booked but not the
predicted amount. Booking *under* the prediction is deliberately not an error.

**Pro-rata absorption**, ranked per worker, labelled an availability measure and kept out of the
wage arithmetic.

### A modelling error a failing test caught

The first cut counted every area with a complement as short by its full norm when nobody stood
in it. A day running one area of five predicted 104 hours of coverage. The recorded decode rules
otherwise in as many words — *"no A2 plating, so no A2 norm to fill"* — so **a norm only binds an
area that ran**, and idle area-days are excluded and reported. The fixture that exposed it was
itself wrong; the failure was worth more than the test.

### Verification

One spec reproduces the 9 Jun decode the rule was ruled on: A1 3/4, A2 3/4, Barrel 3/3, Barrel
pickling 2/2, Pickling A1+A2 2/3 → three sub-areas short one hand each → **24 h expected, 24 h
booked, ₹1,140.00**, character for character the figure in `attendance/2026-W24.md`. A second
reproduces the per-worker split: 4.0 h each for the two-hand pickling crew, 2.7 h for the
three-hand VAT crews, nothing for the barrel-pickling pair who were at norm.

### Still open

The norms are confirmed current (Architect, 27 Aug). What the app cannot do is judge **capacity** —
the rule binds an area at full tilt, and nothing in sep-invoicing measures per-area output. The
capacity index in soma-internal's `operations/attendance-register.md` is the natural input if
that gate is ever wanted.
